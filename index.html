🧮 How It Works

  • Formula: Total Protein = Servings × Grams per Serving. Cost per Gram = Price ÷ Total Protein. Cost per 25g = Cost per Gram × 25.
  • Standardized Dosage (25g): Sports nutrition consensus recommends 20g to 30g of leucine-rich protein post-exercise to maximally stimulate muscle protein synthesis (MPS). Normalizing to 25g provides an objective price-to-benefit comparison.
  • Nutritional Economics: FoodData Central benchmark data shows that lean poultry, bulk whey concentrate, eggs, and canned fish consistently provide the highest protein yield per dollar spent.

Nutritional data sources: USDA FoodData Central; International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) Position Stand: Protein and Exercise.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How is cost per gram of protein calculated?

Total protein content is calculated by multiplying the number of servings by the grams of protein per serving. Total price is then divided by total protein grams: Price ÷ (Servings × Grams of Protein per Serving).

Which protein sources typically offer the lowest cost per gram?

Bulk whey protein concentrate, wholesale chicken breast, canned tuna, and large eggs generally rank as the most cost-effective protein sources, frequently averaging under $0.05 per gram of complete protein.

What is the standard 25g serving cost comparison?

Because standard athletic and clinical protein doses for muscle protein synthesis range between 20g and 30g per meal, our calculator normalizes all foods to a standard 25g protein portion for easy comparison.

Are my grocery prices or meal data saved?

No. All cost math runs locally in your web browser. No grocery inputs, prices, or dietary logs are uploaded or stored.
